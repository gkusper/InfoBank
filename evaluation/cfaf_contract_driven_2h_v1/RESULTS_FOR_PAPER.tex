\paragraph{Results.}
The preregistered study completed 160/160 paired cases: 40/40 on exposed InfoBank development cases, 40/40 on a fixed exposed RefusalBench-NQ fallback, and 80/80 on newly sampled GaRAGe records. The reserved 30 RefusalBench groups were not opened because an existing content-prohibition rule was retained.
On DATASET1, C had 8 non-operational mode matches missed by B and missed 0 matches obtained by B (net 8/40). Intervals jointly resample matched pairs or source groups and do not support a population or significance claim.
On DATASET2, C had 2 non-operational mode matches missed by B and missed 3 matches obtained by B (net -1/40). Intervals jointly resample matched pairs or source groups and do not support a population or significance claim.
The run used 462 gpt-4o-mini calls, 1694395 input tokens and 88615 output tokens, with 0 API errors and no retries, embeddings or external model judges. B reuses A's generation; no extra B inference is charged.
On the fresh 80 GaRAGe rows, C has 71 valid assessments and 9 operational failures. Its 53 FULL and 27 CFAF outputs are behaviors, not accuracy; A/B each produce 71 FULL and 9 CFAF. All systems emit zero actual public citations.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
D1 and D2 are exposed development cohorts; their mode agreement must not be pooled with historical 40-case results or called final answer accuracy. GaRAGe source labels do not certify complete fulfilment or claim entailment. Independent adjudication is pending; 2 of 80 cases use the preregistered whole-passage-prefix context bound. Operational fallbacks remain separate from substantively correct refusals. Exact-copy source associations are unverified and are not rendered as verified claim citations. Public-benchmark familiarity, dependent small samples, existing C short-answer constraints and a single repetition limit generalization. The compact boundary skeleton and future one-call optimization are not evaluated architecture replacements.
